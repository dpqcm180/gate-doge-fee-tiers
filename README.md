## The gap between the price you see and the price you pay

Search for DOGE and you'll get a headline number. That number is the last traded price on some venue, and it is not the price of your order.

Between the two sit four costs, and only one of them is the trading fee:

- **The card or payment processor's cut.** Gate's own buying guide puts card purchases at roughly 1–5%. On a $100 order that's $1 to $5, and it dwarfs everything else.
- **The P2P merchant's spread.** Gate charges 0% platform fee on C2C trades, which sounds free until you notice the merchant sets the price. A slightly worse rate than spot is the real cost.
- **The convert spread.** Swapping USDT to DOGE through the one-click converter is quick, but you're taking whatever rate the engine gives you.
- **The trading fee.** Gate's base tier is 0.10% per side. On $100 that's ten cents.

Which means the honest ranking of what to fix first is: not the trading fee. The card fee and the merchant quote matter more on a first purchase than the fee tier does.

## What you're actually buying

DOGE started in December 2013 as a joke currency built by Billy Markus and Jackson Palmer, using the Shiba Inu meme as a logo. The joke part is often repeated and rarely explained: it was deliberately designed to be fast, cheap and unserious, in contrast to Bitcoin's scarcity story.

That design has a consequence you should know before buying. There's no hard cap on supply. New DOGE is issued on a fixed schedule — historically about 5.2 billion coins a year — which is why DOGE's price behaves differently from a capped asset.

Other things worth knowing:

- One DOGE is divisible, so you can buy a fraction. A $20 order is a completely normal size.
- Its all-time high was $0.7316 on 7 May 2021, per CoinGecko.
- It sits just outside the top ten assets by market cap in recent CoinGecko snapshots, with roughly 155 billion coins circulating.

If you're buying because a chart looked interesting, the supply structure is the part most likely to surprise you later.

### How you actually move up a tier

Three paths run in parallel, and you only need one of them: 30-day trading volume, account asset value, or 14-day average GT holdings. The published thresholds step up like this:

- VIP 1: $60,000 in 30-day volume, or $2,000 in assets
- VIP 5: $1,000,000 in volume, or $40,000 in assets
- VIP 8: $20,000,000 in volume, or $400,000 in assets
- VIP 10: $100,000,000 in volume, or $2,000,000 in assets
- VIP 16: $3,000,000,000 in volume, or $100,000,000 in assets

Volume isn't counted equally across products, which is worth knowing if you're trying to game the threshold. Spot volume — including convert — counts at 100%, the same as stock trading. USDT-margined perpetuals, BTC-margined perpetuals and USDT delivery futures count at 40%. USD1 futures and options count at 20%. CFDs count at 10%. A futures trader and a spot trader with identical notional turnover do not land on the same tier.

Daily withdrawal limits also scale with tier rather than with verification level: $3,000,000 at VIP 0, rising to $50,000,000 at VIP 16.

For a person buying $200 of DOGE, none of this matters. You're at VIP 0, you'll pay 0.10% per side, and you'll pay about twenty cents. The tier system matters if you're trading regularly, which is precisely when it becomes worth checking the live fee page instead of a blog table.

## Questions that come up right before buying

**Do I need to verify my identity?** For most payment channels and for higher limits, yes. Gate's own documentation frames KYC as the thing that unlocks both limits and payment options, and requirements vary by region.

**Can I buy less than one DOGE?** Yes. DOGE is divisible, and small orders are common.

**Can I buy without a card?** Two routes: P2P with a local payment method, or deposit crypto you already own and swap it. Both avoid the 1–5% processor cut.

**How long does it take?** Card purchases usually settle within minutes. Bank transfers run 1–3 business days. On-chain deposits depend on network confirmation.

**Where should I keep it?** Leaving DOGE in the Gate spot wallet means you can trade instantly and withdraw when you want. Self-custody in a hardware wallet means you own the keys, and also that a lost recovery phrase is an unrecoverable mistake. Both are legitimate choices; doing neither deliberately is the mistake.

**Is Gate available where I live?** Not everywhere. Gate restricts services in certain jurisdictions, and the menu of payment options varies by region. Check before you plan around a specific funding route.
